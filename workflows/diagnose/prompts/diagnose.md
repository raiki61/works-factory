## Steps

Steps 1 to 7 are a tree search. Deepen the branches until you are satisfied, and at the end line the branches up against one another.

When to stop deepening a branch: a refutation branch goes until the finding dies, or until it does not die even after reading the neighbourhood and the conditional clauses of the rule. An origin branch goes as far as the label of step 2 (③). An upward-expansion branch goes as far as the three levels of step 3. A prescription branch goes until the deciding factors of step 7 settle the answer on one, or until it hits one of the three cases in which a person is asked. When every tree has come this far, move to lining the branches up.

Deepen a tree rooted at each finding with these branches: (1) refutation (under what conditions it is a false positive; read the neighbourhood and the conditional clauses of the rule to try to kill it, and drop it if you kill it; do this every time, outside the numbering), (2) origin, (3) upward expansion, (4) candidate prescriptions. Build all the trees first, and then line the branches up against one another. Bundle trees that come from the same root (the grain of a unit in step 1). A cause where the upper levels of several trees meet is a candidate for a root countermeasure (the one shot) and is not a reason to bundle units. Check whether the measurement of one tree refutes the finding of another tree. Decide whether a finding is sound only after this cross-check.

1. **Clustering**: Estimate the root cause of the findings by why they were born (their origin), and cut units by the grain below. The grain of a unit is **the shape of a defect on the code** (which line is wrong in what way), and one unit is one shape that one way of fixing closes.
   - You may bundle only when it is another appearance of the same shape (one way of fixing closes all of them, and one `class_query` of step 6 can count all of them), or when one fix to the defective line itself closes both. (What closes at an upper level, such as adding a mechanism or replacing one, belongs to the one shot of step 4 and is not a reason to bundle.)
   - Defects whose places to fix differ, whose shapes differ, and where fixing one does not close the other must be **separate units**, even if the upward expansion of step 3 reaches the same cause at an upper level (the tests do not try the boundary, there is no mechanism that makes a promise hold, the same discipline is missing). A cause that meets at an upper level is not a reason to put units together. Write it in `framing`, in each unit's `why_chain`, and in the one shot of step 4 (`one_shot`, and `one_shot_closes`, which names every unit it closes). Do not reduce the depth of the root (write the upper levels as step 3 says).
   - That the kinds of origin in step 2 (hygiene violation, a dead route, and so on) are the same is not a reason to bundle either.
2. **Origin analysis** (the order is fixed): (1) whether the logic is correct, then (2) whether the origin is poor readability, naming, or a dead route, or a bypass of a standard mechanism, a reinvention of an external standard, a hygiene violation (leftover cleanup, a home-made bypass of an official mechanism, unguarded external I/O, a fail-open verification mechanism), a procedure that does not hold or misfires, or circular grounds, then (3) the label. If the origin is a dead route, a bypass, a hygiene violation, or a reinvention, it is not a "taste" (nit): it causes concrete harm. Reconcile every row of the request without leaving one out. Write it in `origin_analysis`.
3. **Upward expansion**: for each [block] or do-now candidate, ask "why could this defect exist" up to a maximum of 3 levels (so that you do not land on "the culture is bad"). Write it in `why_chain`.
4. **Choose the one shot** (**write it so it can be refuted**): from the causes where the upper levels of several trees meet, choose the one whose fix makes the most findings vanish in a chain (`one_shot`). Do not only look at the branches one by one: **look at combinations too**. Two prescriptions that make each other unnecessary, one that contains the other, and two that close only when both are in place are all candidates for the one shot. Once chosen, **name the keys of the units you expect it to close** in `one_shot_closes`. **A one shot with no names cannot be refuted, and the next round only chooses the same root again.** If no cause meets at an upper level (this includes the case of a single finding), write the fix of the one unit that closes the most into `one_shot` and put that unit's key into `one_shot_closes`. As long as there is even one unit to fix (a unit that step 5 made [block], or a [suggest] with do-now), the acceptance check rejects an empty `one_shot_closes`. Only when there is no unit to fix, `one_shot` is an empty string and `one_shot_closes` is an empty array.
5. **Label and disposition**: give each root-cause unit a label (block, suggest, nit, question, info). Never make a raw finding alone a [block]. A [suggest] gets do-now or defer (the default is do-now; defer only for a structural reason, and a sense of cost is not a reason). Write in `reason` the facts and the refutation you applied in branch (1).
6. **Produce the denominator of the coverage by machine (required for every unit to fix, that is, [block] and do-now)**: do not stop at naming one place of the defect. Write in `class_query` a machine question that can pull out all of "the same shape".
   - Write the question not as a one-line command but **in fields** (`patterns`, `paths`, `count` in `how`, and optionally `fixed`, `untracked`, `ignore_case`, `word`; the shape is as in the output schema). The acceptance check runs this `how` as one `git grep` (no shell and no pipe pass).
   - **Do not write a version.** The acceptance check counts the question on the version it froze, and after the fix it counts the same question again on the working tree (if a version were written into the question, it would keep counting the old version after the fix and the fix would not show in the count).
   - **Write it so that it counts all the lines that become sites to fix.** If you count only the root line, the files of the other lines the fix changes are not in the question's hits, and those sites are listed as "fixes outside the question" and are not counted as coverage.
   - `counts` says which way you count. `defects` is the shape of the defect itself (it decreases when fixed). `population` is the population the fix applies to (it does not decrease when fixed). **Make every hit of a `population` question something the fix changes.** The acceptance check counts it closed only when the fix covered all the hits, so if you count everything a rule applies to (all public functions, say) and mix in things that are already correct, it will not close even when you fix correctly. For a type where the thing that should be there is missing (a missing docstring or test), name and count the missing thing itself (the def line, for example).
   - **When a defective line and a correct line have the same text, change the direction.** If the line to fix and a line you do not fix (a correct line, or a line after a correct fix) are the same text (for example both are `return lo`), the question is applied one line at a time, so in the `defects` direction it would still count the correct line after the fix and never reach 0. In that case make it a `population` question that names a line that can tell the defect apart (the condition line of the branch to fix, for example), so that the hits are only the places to fix. Do not put out a `defects` question that you can tell will not reach 0 (even if you write it in `note`, after the fix an objection comes up and the unit goes to a ruling).
   - **Write the question so that it can be tried on examples.** In `hits` write one line the question should hit (a copy of the defect or population line of the present version), and in `misses` one line it must not hit. For `defects`, put **at least one line after a correct fix** into `misses` (a partial-match question also counts the line after the fix, and the count does not go down whatever the fix does). The acceptance check applies the question to the examples with the same flags it counts with, and rejects the answer if a `hits` line is not hit or a `misses` line is hit. A unit for which you cannot give even one example gets the reason in `examples_unavailable` (do not write it together with examples). A `defects` unit for which you cannot write a line after the fix in one line (a type where the thing that should be there is missing) gets the reason in `misses_omitted_why`. A unit without examples is not rejected, but it is marked "cannot be proven by examples" and shown to a person.
   - If one unit's question lists in `patterns`, one each, defects of different shapes (mistakes that are fixed in different ways), then you have bundled defects of different shapes in one unit: go back to step 1 and split the unit. What does not count as different shapes: a variation in how the same shape is written, a line closed by one fix to the defective line itself, or the sites of one defect (the body line, the place to add an acceptance test, and so on).
   - `total` is replaced by the value the acceptance check counted (a difference is left in `note`). Each of `paths` must hit one or more text files, or the answer is rejected. If there is only one place, write a question that shows it and set `total: 1`.
7. **Enumerate prescriptions from the zero prescription, then the deciding factors you apply yourself, then the world's solutions**: first list the zero prescriptions (withdrawal, or settling it with one existing mechanism), and **after the zero prescription and before any prescription that builds a new mechanism, apply the deciding factors in order.**
   - There are four deciding factors: (a) **a person's earlier decision** (the rows of the recorded policy above: the settled points and the answers of the request), (b) **the person's policy** (the policy text above, if present), (c) **the same scene in the target** (how the code in the target repository solves the same problem; the intent that a comment, docstring, or test description states is not counted in (a) or (b), because it is the target's text and not the owner's decision), (d) **the world's solution** (how the world solves the same problem. If `world_rows` has a row, use that row first: per request row, the class of problem, the established practice, and the comparison with the request's way. Only for a question that has no row, look up primary sources yourself: standard specifications, well-known OSS, official documents).
   - **The way of solving that the request and the purpose sentence showed is not counted as a person's earlier decision** (it is a proposal the factory doubts by comparing it with the world's practice; it is not a deciding factor). What you count in (a) is only what the records show a person decided earlier. If a row of the world's solutions names the request's way "different from the practice", do not leave that difference in the prescription silently: adopt the practice, or write in the prescription a reason for deviating from it (a reason from outside the request).
   - If any of them settles the answer on one, decide it yourself and write it as a prescription in the unit's `prescriptions`. Attach to the reason the source of the deciding factor (its name, and the document and section, or the URL) (the form of "Chosen option: ..., because ..." of the MADR Decision Outcome; for a world's solution, also put it in `precedents` with verdict adopt or adapt). **Do not send to a person what the world's solutions settle.** The same holds for what a previous decision, a policy, or the same scene in the target settles.
   - **You ask a person (fork, escalate) in only these three cases**: (1) the deciding factors disagree and the precedents split (the earlier decisions, the policy, the same scene in the target, and the world's solutions point to different answers), (2) the answer changes with how a person ranks priorities (what to give up as the cost), (3) it conflicts with a row of the recorded policy above (following the deciding factors would overturn that row).
   - A conflict with the target's text is not case (3). If the target's text disagrees with the clean shape, put in the prescription the site that rewrites that text (the same shape of defect as "consistency between the setting comment and the implementation value, and file paths in the PR description" in the source of the review viewpoints).
   - Put into `questions` as `kind: fork` ("a fork in the design") only a prescription that reaches a new mechanism or a widened shared surface and that falls in one of the three cases. Write the consequence and the cost of each of its `options`, and add one sentence to `reason` saying why the zero prescription falls short (if you cannot, it is not a fork: settle it with the zero prescription). **At the end of `reason` always write this role's recommendation** in the form "推し: <option>——<reason>", with the mark `推し:` written as these Japanese characters whatever the language of the reply (the machine finds the recommendation by that mark; it is put as it is in the sentence that asks the person, and if the person's answer does not touch the question, it is fixed with that recommendation).
   - **If you adopt a prescription that reduces or narrows a capability that existed in BASE, write the capability that is lost in the unit's `reason`**: this role does not decide whether to take it as a cost. It goes through the fix plan's narrows and the pre-review, and the run asks a person before the fix.
   - Do not embed the place of the fix in a defect's key (write it in the shape of the symptom).
8. **The rows of precedents (`precedents`)**: one row per key of a unit to fix ([block] and do-now) and per key of a question sent to a person (fork and escalate): the name of the generalized problem (`problem`), the primary source (`source`: a URL, or a document and section), the conclusion (`verdict`: adopt = take as it is, adapt = take with modifications, does_not_apply = does not apply, not_found = could not find), and the reason (`reason`).
   - **Use the world's solutions first**: if `world_rows` has a row of a class that overlaps the place of that unit, `problem` is that class's one-sentence problem, `source` is `{world_prefix}<the class id>` (the row's excerpt of its source is held by that row), and `verdict` says whether you take that practice (adopt, adapt) or it does not apply (does_not_apply). Only for a unit or question that has no row, look it up yourself. The acceptance check rejects the answer if a `{world_prefix}<class id>` is not in the rows of the world's solutions.
   - Look up primary sources with WebSearch and WebFetch. Write what you looked up in `source` as a source (a URL, or a document and section), and if you looked and found nothing, use `not_found`. **If it is not_found, always write what you searched for and how (`searched`)** (the acceptance check rejects the answer without it).
   - **A row for a question sent to a person carries `undecided_because`** (why the deciding factors of step 7 did not settle it, at least {undecided_min} characters). Say which of the three cases in which a person is asked in step 7 it falls in (1 the precedents split, 2 the answer changes with the priorities, 3 it conflicts with a row of the recorded policy), by **starting `undecided_because` with a mark (①, ②, ③; if several apply, line them up)**, and then list the sources of the deciding factors that applied. If you write ③ in the head mark, copy the naming of the row it conflicts with from the recorded policy above as it is (the acceptance check rejects a question that has ③ in its head mark but names no row of the list; a ③ inside the body text does not count as claiming it). **If you cannot write it, it is obvious, so do not send it to a person** (take it as a prescription).
9. **Stable keys**: "file path + symbol name + a one-sentence summary". For the same defect, use the same key string (matching is by exact string equality).

## Question ledger (questions)

This section is the one place that holds the rules of the ledger.

- The only fields you may write are `key`, `kind`, `status`, `reason`, `resolution`, `options`, `depends`, and `origin`.
- `kind`, with what the origin must be and the field each needs:
  - `fork` (origin is the key of the unit concerned; `options` is required): a fork in the design. The prescription reaches a new mechanism or a widened shared surface, there are several candidates, and even after consulting the world's solutions (standard specifications and the established practice of well-known OSS) it is not decided (step 7, enumerating prescriptions, and the "minimality of the prescription" of the source of the review viewpoints). If the world's solutions decide it, it is not a fork: take it as a prescription. At least two options, with the consequences written. Why the zero prescription (withdrawal, or one existing mechanism) falls short goes in `reason`.
  - `split` (origin is the key of the unit concerned): an existing defect that the fix exposed, outside the frozen purpose. A person decides whether to stack it in another PR. Collecting what was exposed stays the default (the source of the review viewpoints: "do not make deferral to a separate issue the default"); this is an application for an exception.
  - `rule` (origin is the key of the unit concerned): the same finding flares up again with no new evidence, and the cause is not the code but a misfire of a viewpoint. Write in `reason` which viewpoint of the review source it is.
  - `stuck` (origin is the key of the unit concerned): whether the prescription was wrong or it is a design problem.
  - `thrash` (it can hold neither origin nor depends): new [block]s keep appearing and the rounds do not converge. It is seen from the trend of counts and is tied to no particular unit.
  - `premise` (origin is one of R1 to R4): R2 said the premise is invalid. Whether a judge in a separate context checked the premise against reality is the content of the rejudgment.
  - `unverifiable` (origin is one of R1 to R4): an independent reviewer R cannot take the material to check.
  - `awaiting` (origin is the name of a material): the material is awaiting_human (unobserved, a list that was cut off, a decision record that could not be washed, or a CI that cannot be run).
  - `field` (it can hold neither origin nor depends): it is not decided until a person checks it in the field (real machines, outside environments, operations that need permission), and no material is awaiting_human. Do not borrow a material's field: if you borrow the CI field, the step that reruns the CI overwrites it every round. It does not stop the round, and it goes at the head of the final report.
- `status`, with the fields each needs:
  - `held`: undecided. The judge of the next round rejudges it (a fork can make its origin [block] wait for one round only). If you open a question in this round, use held.
  - `decided` (`resolution` is required): the loop decided it, but the defect of its origin is still open.
  - `resolved` (`resolution` is required): the loop decided it and its origin also closed; it may be dropped from the next round's ledger.
  - `escalate`: after rejudgment, only a person can decide it (taste, policy, a hard-to-reverse agreement, rewriting the purpose).
- `split` and `rule` cannot take a [block] or a do-now unit as origin or depends (the duty to fix would vanish before a person is asked). Make the unit defer and write a structural reason.
- If a material is awaiting_human, always put a question of `kind: awaiting` whose origin is that material (the acceptance check rejects an answer without one).
- **A question does not remove an obstacle**: the unit concerned stays [block].
- If you put in the ledger the same question that the `answers` of the request answered, make its key the answer's `question` character for character. A question whose text differs is tied with `{{answer, to: <the question's key>}}` of `answer_ties`; if you do not tie it, the answer matches nothing and the acceptance check rejects the answer. A question of `awaiting` whose origin is a material you keep putting as it is while the material is awaiting_human (the answer matches by that origin). For a `field` question that a person must check when a material is not run (not_run) (a field cannot hold an origin), begin its key with the material's name (`<material name>: <what to check>`): the answers of the request are written under the material's name, and the machine reads the material name at the head of a key as the origin and ties the answer.
- For a `field` or `awaiting` question, if there is a command that settles it when a person types it by hand, write it in `reason` as "How to measure: `<command>`" (only when there is such a command; a person types it, so limit it to commands that only read: do not write a command that writes, sends outside, or cannot be undone). `options` is the field for choices, so do not write a command in it.

## How to write answer_ties

Tie the answers listed in `answers` of the materials file, and the rows of the section "What the previous round's independent eye said needs a redesign", to the questions and units of the present reply, in the rows of `answer_ties`. There are four shapes of row:

- `{{answer, to}}`: `answer` is the answer's `question` copied without changing a single character. `to` is the `key` of a question or the `key` of a unit of the present reply (tied to a question it means the question is answered; tied to a unit it means an answer to that unit).
- `{{answer, none}}`: when no question or unit matches the answer, write in `none` the reason it matches nothing, at least {tie_min} characters (it is listed with its reason in the report).
- `{{redesign, to}}`: `redesign` is the `text` of the row of that section up to the first "（" (the fullwidth opening parenthesis). `to` is the `key` of a [block] or do-now unit.
- `{{redesign, none}}`: when the row was killed by refutation or is outside the frozen purpose, write the reason in `none`, at least {tie_min} characters (it is not carried to the next run, and it is listed in the report).

The acceptance check asks for a tie for an answer that matches no question of the reply by character equality, for an answer that matches several questions, and for every row of the section "What the previous round's independent eye said needs a redesign". (An answer that matches exactly one question whose `key` is the answer's `question` character for character, and an answer written under the name of a material that was not measured, need not be written; if you write one, the tie wins.) It rejects the answer if `to` is not a question or unit of the present reply, if you tie a row to be redone to a defer unit, or if the reason of a `none` is short. The acceptance check looks at the ties only at the time it accepts the judgment's reply; if a later rejudgment lowers a unit, it is named in the report of the rejudgment.
