# Decide which way an approval note points

**Core rule: a note that only adds to the plan's way of doing things keeps the plan; a note that asks for a different way, behaviour, or scope changes it. When part of the note differs, it changes the plan; when you doubt, it is not "aligned".**

A person who answers "continue" and adds a few words has approved the plan as it stands — unless those few words ask for something else. Carrying out the plan as written while the note asked for something different does not do what the person asked, and it looks like approval in the record.

## Pitfalls (read first)

- **Judge only from what you have**: the approved plan, the items that were shown to the person when they answered, and the note. Do not fill in what the person "must have meant" from anything else.
- **Partly aligned and partly different is redirected.** A note that agrees with most of the plan but changes one item still changes the plan.
- **Doubt is not alignment.** A note that is too vague, contradicts itself, or depends on something you were not given is unclear — never aligned by default.
- **Compare with each item.** Read the approach of each planned change, and the items that were put to the person.

## Steps

### What counts as which

- **Aligned**: the note agrees with the plan's direction. It may add a condition, a caution, a scope limit, or a reason; carrying out the plan as written still does what the person asked.
- **Redirected**: the note chooses a different direction from the plan or from an item that was shown — a different behaviour, approach, or scope. Carrying out the plan as written would not do what the person asked.
- **Unclear**: you cannot tell which way the note points.

## Done when

- You compared the note with the approach of each planned change and with each item that was shown.
- The verdict is aligned, redirected, or unclear; a note that differs in part gave redirected, and doubt gave unclear, not aligned.
- The sentence names the item and what the note asks, and quotes the note's words as they are.

## How to report

Give the verdict, and one sentence that names the plan item or shown item the note agrees with or differs from, and what the note asks. Quote the note's words as they are.

## Example

Input: the plan's item 1 retries an upload three times and then stops with an error. The note says "continue, but never give up — keep retrying until it goes through".

Reply: Redirected. Item 1 stops with an error after three tries, and the note asks to "keep retrying until it goes through", which is a different behaviour.

## Works well with

- A way of writing the items put to a person so that a short answer to them can be read without guessing.
