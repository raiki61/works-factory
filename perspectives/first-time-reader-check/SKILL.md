# Read a text as a first-time reader in the middle of other work

**Core rule: read only the text, with no context, and stop at every place you cannot take the meaning. One stop or one guess means the text needs rewriting.**

The person who reads the text reads it while switching between several tasks. They do not carry the conversation it came from, and they decide from the top whether to read on.

## Pitfalls (read first)

- **Read only the text.** You know nothing of where it came from or what it is for. Do not look anything up to understand it.
- **Do not fill a gap with a guess and read on as if it were said.** Write the guess down as a guess.

## Steps

'What to check' applies to every text; when you are given only the head of a report, also read 'When you check only the head of a report'.

### What to check

- **The first three lines**: do they alone let the reader say what this is about, whether it concerns them, and whether it is urgent? If not, note where that became impossible.
- **References that lean on something outside the text**: "the earlier one", "as discussed", "that issue" — every reference that relies on context before or after this text is a stop.
- **Bare symbols**: every place that shows only a symbol, an abbreviation, or a path without saying what it is, is a stop.
- **Any other place** you could not take the meaning.

### When you check only the head of a report

- **Read only the head.** Judge from what the head says, not from what you know of the rest.
- **The head also says what to decide.** Besides what it is about, whether it concerns the reader, and whether it is urgent, check that the first lines let the reader say what they are asked to decide.
- **Pointing to a section is not a stop.** Where the head names the sections below by their headings and leaves detailed numbers, paths, and history to them, do not count it as a stop.

## Done when

- You checked the first three lines, every reference that leans on something outside the text, and every bare symbol, abbreviation, or path.
- Every stop and every guess is written down, each naming its place in the text.
- The verdict follows the count: one stop or one guess means rewriting; none means it passes.

## How to report

- **Stops**: each place you stopped because you could not take the meaning, one sentence each, naming the place in the text.
- **Guesses**: each thing you filled in by guessing, one sentence each, naming the place.
- **Decidable**: whether a decision can be made from this text alone.
- **Verdict**: if there is even one stop or one guess, the text needs rewriting; if there is none, it passes.

Keep quoted words from the text, identifiers, and paths as they are.

## Example

Input: "Fixed as agreed in the earlier thread. Details in WR-7. Merging tomorrow."

Reply:
- Stops: line 1 does not say what was fixed; "the earlier thread" leans on a conversation outside the text; "WR-7" is an abbreviation that is never explained.
- Guesses: none.
- Decidable: no.
- Verdict: needs rewriting.

## Works well with

- A perspective that sets the stance of a reader who has no tools and no context.
